Offline Japanese Translation for Your iPhone Trip to Japan

You can translate in Japan without internet if you download Japanese and your own language over Wi‑Fi before you leave. Then test everything in Airplane Mode. I’ll use Konjac Translate, the app I made, to show what to set up and what works offline. I’ll also share useful phrases for five travel situations and explain what the app can’t do, such as translating a menu with your camera.

1. Four things to do over Wi‑Fi before you leave

Offline translation depends on languages already downloaded to your phone. If you discover something is missing after you arrive in Japan, you may not have a reliable connection to download it. Do these four things over your home Wi‑Fi a day or two before you leave:

  1. Download Japanese translation: In Konjac Translate, open the “Settings” tab, tap “Offline languages,” find Japanese, and tap “Download.” Apple will show a download consent screen. The download starts after you agree. Both Japanese and your own language must be listed under “Translation built in · works offline” for offline translation in both directions.
  2. Download the Japanese speech model: After you select Japanese as part of your language pair, the app downloads its speech recognition model. Each language takes about 150–400 MB. Downloads use Wi‑Fi only and can take a few minutes to half an hour. You’re done when Japanese shows “Speech ready” in “Offline languages.”
  3. Download a natural Japanese voice: If spoken translations sound robotic, you haven’t downloaded a natural voice yet. On your iPhone, go to “Settings” > “Accessibility” > “Read & Speak” (called “Spoken Content” on older iOS versions) > “Voices.” Tap “Japanese” and download a voice marked “Premium.” If there’s no “Premium” option, choose “Enhanced.” Konjac Translate will switch to it automatically when you return to the app.
  4. Test it in Airplane Mode: Turn off your connection. In the “Talk” tab, say “Where is the restroom?” Make sure the app understands you, translates the question into Japanese, and reads it aloud.

Apple manages these translation models, so other apps can use the same downloads. To delete them, go to your iPhone’s “Settings” > “Apps” > “Translate” > “Downloaded Languages.”

3. Trains and transit: Tickets, platforms, and transfers

When taking a train in Japan, you may need to ask where to buy a ticket, which platform to use, where to transfer, or when the last train leaves. Station staff are often busy. Showing a prepared card can be quicker than speaking a long question into your phone. Konjac Translate has these phrases under “Transport” in “Travel cards”:

SituationEnglish on the cardApproximate Japanese
Finding ticketsWhere can I buy a ticket?切符はどこで買えますか？
Checking the platformWhich platform does this train leave from?この電車は何番線から出ますか？
TransferringWhere do I need to change trains?どこで乗り換えればいいですか？
Last trainWhat time is the last train?終電は何時ですか？
Taking a taxiPlease take me to this address. Thank you.この住所までお願いします。

The Japanese on the right is what I used as a reference when writing the cards. The translation you see in the app comes from Apple’s translation models on your iPhone. The wording may differ slightly, but the meaning is the same.

For a taxi, save your hotel’s Japanese address as your own card and show it to the driver. Don’t rely on saying it aloud. If the driver or station staff answers, switch to “Talk” so they can speak Japanese into your phone. Then read the English translation.

4. Restaurants and convenience stores: Seating, allergies, and paying

You can often order by pointing at a picture. What you may need to explain is whether you need a table or can’t eat a particular food. “Dining” has these cards:

The built-in allergy card says you have a food allergy, but it doesn’t name the allergen. Before your trip, add a card that spells it out, such as “I’m allergic to shrimp. Please don’t include shrimp.” Translate it into Japanese in Konjac Translate and enlarge it to show the staff. The free version lets you save 3 of your own cards. Start with essentials like an allergy and your hotel address.

To find a convenience store, use “Is there a convenience store nearby?” under “Directions.” If someone speaks too quickly, “Basics” has “Could you speak a little more slowly?” and “Could you write it down for me?” You can type what they write into the “Translate” tab to get an English translation.

5. Shopping, directions, and emergencies

Shopping: “Shopping” has “How much does this cost?”, “Can I pay by credit card?”, “Can I get a tax refund here?”, and “Could I have a bag, please?” Follow official Japanese guidance and the store’s instructions for tax refund rules and procedures. Translation just helps you ask the question.

Directions: If you’re lost, show “Excuse me, I’m lost,” followed by “Could you show me on the map?” Open your map app so the person can point. That’s easier than trying to follow a long set of spoken directions.

Emergencies: “Emergency” has phrases including “Please call an ambulance,” “Please call the police,” “I’ve lost my passport,” and “Is there a pharmacy nearby?” Save these numbers listed by the Japan National Tourism Organization too:

  • Police: 110
  • Fire and ambulance: 119
  • Japan Visitor Hotline: 050-3816-2787 (+81-50-3816-2787 when calling from overseas). It operates 24 hours a day, year-round, with service in English, Chinese, and Korean. It can help with accidents, illness, natural disasters, and general travel questions.

In a real emergency, call for help first. A translation app is only a support tool.

6. What Konjac Translate can’t do

  1. No camera translation: Konjac Translate doesn’t have a camera feature. You can’t take a picture of a menu, sign, or ticket machine to translate it. For that, you can use the camera feature in Apple’s “Translate” app or Google Translate. Check each app’s official guidance to see whether its camera translation works offline.
  2. You need internet before your trip: You must download Japanese translation, the speech model, and the voice first. You can’t complete the initial setup without internet.
  3. iPhone only: The app requires iOS 18 or later. There’s no Android version.
  4. Ads in the free version: A small banner appears at the top of the screen. On the “Talk” screen, it appears only on your side, not on the half facing the other person. Watching a video hides ads for 15 minutes. Pro has no ads.

7. Common questions

Can a translation app really work completely offline in Japan?

Yes, if you download Japanese and your own language before you leave. In Konjac Translate, both languages need to be listed under “Translation built in · works offline,” and Japanese needs to show “Speech ready.” Translation, speech recognition, and spoken translations then run on your iPhone without internet.

How large is the Japanese speech model? Can I download it over cellular?

Each language’s speech recognition model is about 150–400 MB. iOS downloads it over Wi‑Fi only, so it stays at 0% on cellular. Download it over your home Wi‑Fi before you leave.

Do travel cards work without internet?

Yes. Travel cards use your downloaded languages to translate phrases into Japanese on your iPhone. Tap a card to translate it, then enlarge it to show someone.

Can I take a picture of a menu to translate it?

Konjac Translate doesn’t have a camera feature, so it can’t translate photos. For menus or signs, you can use the camera feature in Apple’s “Translate” app or Google Translate.

What number should I call in an emergency in Japan?

According to the Japan National Tourism Organization, call 110 for police or 119 for fire and ambulance services. The Japan Visitor Hotline, 050-3816-2787, operates 24 hours a day, year-round, and offers service in Chinese.

For other questions, see the support page.
