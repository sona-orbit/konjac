Type in English, Reply in Any Language with iPhone Keyboard

The hardest part of texting in another language is not looking up words. It is expressing your exact meaning. I built this translation keyboard with a simple approach: type naturally in the language you know best first. When you switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard, the translation is already ready. Below it, you will see three AI variations: casual, polite, and concise. Pick the one that fits best, tap to replace your text, and hit send. If you receive a message you cannot read, copy it and switch to the keyboard to see the English translation and suggested replies. This guide explains why this workflow works, how to set it up, and how to use it.

1. Why write in your native language first

Typing directly in a foreign language often leads to sentences cobbled together from words you happen to know. The general meaning might be there, but the tone feels off, details go missing, or it sounds stiff. When you write in your native language, you know exactly what you want to say, how polite you need to be, and what to emphasize.

Here is how the translation keyboard works:

  1. Write your thought clearly in your native language first. Use your regular keyboard and type the way you normally talk.
  2. Switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard, and the translation is already done. The keyboard reads what you just typed (or the text you selected) and translates it directly on your iPhone.
  3. Pick a better phrasing. Below the translation, you will see three AI versions: casual, polite, and concise. They carry the same meaning with different tones. Pick the one that fits your recipient best, tap it, and it replaces your text.

You do not need to write flawless foreign text. You only need to know what you want to say. Browsing the options also helps you learn how the same idea is phrased in that language.

2. First-time setup: three toggles

Open 翻譯蒟蒻 and go to the “Translation Keyboard” section under the “Settings” tab. That screen is a checklist that automatically checks off each step as you complete it. Tapping “Open Settings” takes you directly to the iPhone settings page for 翻譯蒟蒻, where you will find the three items below:

  1. Add the keyboard: Go to “Settings” > “General” > “Keyboard” > “Keyboards” > “Add New Keyboard…”, and choose “翻譯蒟蒻”. (the keyboard is listed under its Chinese name)
  2. Enable “Allow Full Access”: Tap “翻譯蒟蒻” and turn on “Allow Full Access”. This is needed to read the clipboard and use your own AI API key. It is not required if you only translate selected text.
  3. Set “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow”: Go to “Settings” > “Apps” > “翻譯蒟蒻” > “Paste from Other Apps”. If you leave this unchanged, iOS will ask for permission every time you copy text and switch over (see section 5).

Once configured, there is a practice area at the bottom of the same screen in the app. Tap “Copy Japanese phrase”, tap the text field, hold the globe key to switch to “Konjac Translate”, and you will see it in action right away while finishing the checklist.

You also need to download your languages in the app first. The keyboard only translates between language pairs you have already downloaded, matching the two languages selected in the app. To switch to a different target language, tap the language label in the top left corner of the keyboard.

3. Replying: type naturally, then pick a better phrasing

  1. Use your regular keyboard to type what you want to say in English in your chat box, like in WhatsApp or Messages.
  2. Hold the globe key and select “Konjac Translate”. The keyboard grabs the text before the cursor (or whatever text you selected), and the translation appears immediately.
  3. Check the three phrasing options below: Casual sounds like texting a friend, Polite fits coworkers or new contacts, and Concise keeps words to a minimum while preserving meaning.
  4. Tap the one that fits best, and your English text is replaced with that translation. If you just want the direct translation, tap “Replace”. Review it, then hit send.

If you tap one option and change your mind, tapping another replaces it cleanly instead of stacking text. To restore your original text, tap “Undo” in the bottom left corner. If you want the recipient to see both languages, tap “Insert Below”. This keeps your original text and adds the translation on the next line.

The keyboard determines the direction automatically: if the text is in your language, it translates into the foreign language; if it is foreign text, it translates into your language for you to read (section 4). When there is no text, it acts as an English keyboard. To type in another script, hold the globe key to switch back to your normal keyboard.

4. Reading incoming messages: copy and switch

  1. Touch and hold the incoming message and tap “Copy”.
  2. Tap the input field and switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard.
  3. The keyboard displays the English meaning. It only shows it to you and does not paste it into the text box, so you never accidentally send a translation.

The “Suggested Replies” section below offers three ready-made responses in the other person’s language, each with an English explanation, like “Sounds good”, “Can we reschedule?”, or “Ask a question”. Tap one to insert it into the text box, edit if needed, and send. The keyboard automatically detects the language of the copied text. If they send Japanese, it translates Japanese; if Korean, it translates Korean, provided the language is downloaded.

5. What to do if “Allow Paste” keeps popping up

To protect your clipboard, iOS asks “Allow Paste?” whenever an app tries to read content copied from another app. Keyboards are subject to the same rule, and iOS defaults to asking every time.

Before you change this setting, Konjac Translate does not automatically read your clipboard when you switch over. Instead, it asks: “New text found on clipboard. Translate it?”. It only reads the clipboard after you tap to confirm, which is when iOS shows the prompt. This prevents unwanted system popups when you just wanted to type.

If you want copied text translated immediately upon switching, go to “Settings” > “Apps” > “Konjac Translate” and change “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow”. Once the keyboard detects that permission is granted, it translates automatically next time.

6. Privacy: what the keyboard reads and what it does not

  • It only reads when you switch to it. It reads selected text, text before the cursor, or copied text (when Full Access is on), solely to translate it.
  • It does not log your keystrokes. The keyboard never saves, logs, or uploads what you type.
  • Translations happen on your iPhone. It uses Apple on-device translation models, which work completely offline once languages are downloaded.
  • AI variations prioritize Apple Intelligence, running directly on your iPhone. If your iPhone does not support Apple Intelligence, you can enter your own AI API key under “Settings” > “AI” in the app. Only in that case is the text sent directly from your iPhone to your chosen AI service, never passing through me.
  • Password fields always use the system keyboard. Under iOS rules, third-party keyboards are never shown in secure password fields.

For complete details, see the Translation Keyboard section in the Privacy Policy.

7. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay for the translation keyboard?

No. The translation keyboard and AI phrasing options are available in the free version. AI phrasing requires an iPhone with Apple Intelligence or your own AI API key configured in the app (any API usage fees are billed directly by that AI provider).

Does it work offline?

Yes, for translation, as long as the language pair has been downloaded in the app. AI phrasing powered by Apple Intelligence also runs locally on your iPhone. An internet connection is only needed if you use your own AI API key.

Why does it require “Allow Full Access”?

Full Access is required to read the clipboard and connect to your own AI service. If you only translate selected text or text before the cursor and rely on Apple Intelligence, the keyboard works without Full Access.

Can I type in other languages using this keyboard?

No. When there is no text to translate, it only provides an English keyboard layout. To type in another language, hold the globe key to switch to your regular keyboard, type your text, and then switch over to translate.

What if a translation is wrong or I replaced the wrong text?

Tap “Undo” in the bottom left corner of the keyboard to restore your original text.
