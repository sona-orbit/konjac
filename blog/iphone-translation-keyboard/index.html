iPhone Translation Keyboard: Type in Your Language First

Texting in another language is tough. The hardest part is not looking up words, but getting your actual meaning across. I built the translation keyboard around a simple idea: type first in your native language, the way you normally talk. Switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard, and the translation is already done. Below it, you will see three AI rewrites: casual, polite, and short. Pick the best fit, tap once to replace your text, and send. If you cannot read an incoming message, copy it and switch over to see the translation and suggested replies. Here is why this workflow works, how to set it up, and how to use it.

1. Why type in your native language first

When you try typing directly in a foreign language, sentences often end up patched together from whatever words you remember. The general idea might come across, but the tone feels wrong, nuances get lost, or it sounds stiff. When you type in your native language, you know exactly what you want to say, how polite you need to be, and what to emphasize.

That is why the translation keyboard uses this workflow:

  1. Write clearly in your own language first. Use your regular keyboard and write naturally.
  2. Switch to Konjac Translate, and the translation is ready. The keyboard reads what you just typed (or the text you selected) and translates it directly on your iPhone.
  3. Pick a better rewrite. Under the translation, you get three AI-generated options: casual, polite, and short. They keep the same meaning with different tones. Tap the one that fits best, and it replaces your text instantly.

You do not need to be an expert writer in another language. You only need to know what you want to say. As you pick a rewrite, you also learn how native speakers express that same thought.

2. First-time setup: all in one place

Open 翻譯蒟蒻 and go to the “Settings” tab, then tap “Translation Keyboard”. That screen is a checklist that automatically checks off each item as you finish it. Tap “Open Settings” to jump directly to 翻譯蒟蒻’s page inside the iPhone Settings app. All three steps live there:

  1. Tap “Keyboards” and turn on “翻譯蒟蒻”: this adds the keyboard without digging through General settings.
  2. Turn on “Allow Full Access” on the same page: this is needed to read your clipboard and use your own AI API keys. If you only translate selected text, it works without this.
  3. Go back one screen and set “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow”: if you leave this as default, iOS will ask for permission every time you copy text and switch keyboards (see section 5).

You can also do this from any app: touch and hold the Globe key in the bottom-left corner of any text field, then tap “Keyboard Settings…” at the top to jump straight to keyboard settings.

Once set up, use the practice field at the bottom of that checklist screen. Tap “Copy a Japanese sentence”, tap the text field, hold the Globe key, select “翻譯蒟蒻”, and see it work right away.

Make sure to download your languages inside the app first. The keyboard only translates downloaded language pairs, following the two languages selected in the app. To switch the target language, tap the language label in the top-left corner of the keyboard.

3. Replying: type in your language, then pick a better rewrite

  1. Using your regular keyboard, type what you want to say in English inside any chat box, like WhatsApp or Messages.
  2. Touch and hold the Globe key and pick “Konjac Translate”. The keyboard grabs the text before the cursor (or select a specific part first), and the translation appears immediately.
  3. Check the three rewrites below: Casual sounds like texting a friend, Polite works for clients or new acquaintances, and Short keeps things brief but complete.
  4. Tap the best fit, and your English text gets replaced by that translated line. If you prefer the literal translation, tap “Replace”. Review it, then tap send.

If you tap one option and change your mind, tapping another replaces it cleanly without stacking text. To restore your original text, tap “Undo” in the bottom-left corner. If you want the recipient to see both languages, tap “Insert After” to keep the original and place the translation on the next line.

The keyboard detects translation direction automatically: it translates your language into theirs, and foreign text into your language (see section 4). When there is no text to translate, it acts as an English keyboard. To type in another script, switch back to your regular keyboard using the Globe key.

4. Reading incoming messages: copy, then switch

  1. Touch and hold the incoming message and tap “Copy”.
  2. Tap the text field and switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard.
  3. The keyboard displays the translation in your language. It only shows it on the keyboard screen and does not paste it into the text box, so you never send it by mistake.

Below that, “Suggested Replies” gives you three ready-to-send responses in the sender’s language, each paired with an English translation, such as “Sounds good”, “Can we reschedule?”, or “Quick question”. Tap one to insert it into the chat box, edit if needed, and send. The keyboard automatically detects the language, whether it is Japanese or Spanish, as long as that language pack is downloaded.

5. Stopping the “Allow Paste” prompt

To protect your clipboard, iOS asks “Allow Paste?” whenever an app reads content copied from another app. Third-party keyboards trigger the same prompt, and iOS asks every time by default.

Until you update that setting, the Konjac Translate keyboard will not read your clipboard the moment you switch over. Instead, it asks: “New copied text found. Translate it?” It only reads the clipboard after you tap that prompt, which is when iOS shows its permission dialog. This prevents unexpected popups when you just want to type.

To have it translate automatically whenever you switch over, open “Settings” > “Apps” > “Konjac Translate”, and change “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow”. Once the keyboard detects this permission, it translates clipboard text automatically next time.

6. Privacy: what the keyboard reads and what it never touches

  • It only reads when you switch to it. It reads selected text, text before the cursor, or copied text (when Full Access is on), strictly to translate it.
  • No keystroke logging. The keyboard never saves, logs, or uploads what you type.
  • Translation happens on your iPhone. It uses Apple on-device translation models, running fully offline once languages are downloaded.
  • AI rewrites prioritize Apple Intelligence, running directly on your iPhone. If your iPhone does not support Apple Intelligence, you can enter your own AI API key under “Settings” > “AI”. Only in that case is text sent directly from your iPhone to your chosen AI provider, never through my servers.
  • Password fields always use the system keyboard. iOS enforces this rule. Custom keyboards never appear in password fields.

7. FAQ

Is the translation keyboard free?

Yes. The translation keyboard and AI rewrites work in the free version. AI rewrites require an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, or your own AI API key entered in the app (usage fees are billed directly by your AI provider).

Does it work offline?

Translation works offline as long as both languages are downloaded in the app. Apple Intelligence rewrites also generate on-device. You only need an internet connection if you use your own AI API key.

Why does it need "Allow Full Access"?

Full Access is required to read your clipboard and connect to third-party AI services. If you only translate selected text or text before the cursor using Apple Intelligence, it works without Full Access.

Can I type in other languages directly on this keyboard?

No. When there is no text to translate, it only provides standard English input. To type in other languages, tap the Globe key to switch back to your native keyboard, then switch over to translate.

What if a translation or replacement is wrong?

Tap “Undo” in the bottom-left corner of the keyboard to restore your original text.
