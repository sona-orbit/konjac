Write in Your Language, Reply in Theirs: How to Use an iPhone Translation Keyboard

I made the translation keyboard for a simple reason: when you reply to someone in another language, you should be able to write in the language you know best. Type what you want to say, then switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard. Your translation is ready. Tap “Replace,” and your message changes to the other person's language, ready to send. If you want a different tone, you can choose from three AI-written options: casual, polite, or brief. If you can't understand a message you received, copy it and switch to the keyboard to see its meaning and suggested replies. Here's how to set everything up and use it.

This article covers the translation keyboard in my app. Button and setting names reflect the current app and iPhone running iOS 18 or later, as checked on November 20, 2026. 1. Write in your language, send in theirs

When you reply to a friend or client who speaks another language, you know what you want to say. You just need it in their language. The translation keyboard puts that step in the text field you're already using. You don't need to copy your message into a translation app and paste it back:

  1. Write in your language.Use your usual keyboard and type the way you normally would.
  2. Switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard.It reads what you just typed, or the text you selected, and translates it on your iPhone.
  3. Tap “Replace,” then send.Your message changes to the other person's language in the same text field.

Below the translation, you'll also find three AI-written versions: casual, polite, and brief. They keep the same meaning but change the tone. Tap one if it fits better.

2. First-time setup: one page, three settings

Open 翻譯蒟蒻 and go to “Translation Keyboard” on the “Settings” tab. That page has a checklist that checks off each step as you finish it. Tap “Open Settings” to jump to 翻譯蒟蒻's page in iPhone “Settings.” All three settings are there:

  1. Tap “Keyboards” and turn on “翻譯蒟蒻”: This adds the keyboard. You don't need to look for it under “General.”
  2. On the same page, turn on “Allow Full Access”: The keyboard needs this to read the clipboard and use your own AI key. You can translate selected text without it.
  3. Go back one page and set “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow”: Otherwise, iPhone will ask each time you copy new text and switch to the keyboard (see section 5).

You can also get to keyboard settings from another app. In any text field, press and hold the globe key at the lower left, then choose “Keyboard Settings…” at the top.

Once you're set up, try the practice field at the bottom of the same page. Tap “Copy a Spanish sentence,” tap the text field, then hold the globe key and choose “翻譯蒟蒻” to see it work.

You'll also need to download languages in the app first. The keyboard translates only language pairs you've downloaded, in the direction set by the two languages selected in the app. To translate into another language, tap the language at the upper left of the keyboard.

3. Reply: type, switch keyboards, and replace

  1. Use your usual keyboard to type your reply in English in a WhatsApp text field.
  2. Press and hold the globe key, then choose “Konjac Translate.” The keyboard picks up the text before your cursor and shows the translation. To translate a specific passage or a message with several sentences, select it first.
  3. Tap “Replace.” Your English changes to the other person's language. Check it, then send.

For a different tone, look at the three options below. Casual sounds like a message to a friend. Polite suits someone you just met or a client. Brief uses fewer words while keeping the meaning. Tap an option to use it. If you tap another, it replaces the first instead of adding to it. To get your original English back, tap “Undo” at the lower left. If you want the other person to see your English too, tap “Insert After.” The original stays, with the translation on the next line.

The keyboard figures out the direction: text in your language goes into the other person's language, while text in another language is translated for you to read (see section 4). When there's no text to translate, it becomes an English keyboard. To type in another language, use the globe key to return to your usual keyboard.

4. Don't understand a message? Copy it

  1. Press and hold the other person's message, then choose “Copy.”
  2. Tap the text field and switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard.
  3. The keyboard shows what the message means in English. It doesn't put the translation in the text field, so you won't send it by accident.

Below that, “Suggested Replies” offers three replies written in the other person's language, each with its meaning in English. They might say “Yes,” “Could we change the time?” or ask a question. Tap one to put it in the text field, edit it, and send it. The keyboard detects the copied text's language automatically. If they write in Spanish, it translates Spanish; if they write in French, it translates French, as long as you've downloaded that language.

5. How to stop the paste permission prompt

To protect your clipboard, iPhone asks before an app reads something you copied from another app. The keyboard is subject to the same prompt, which appears every time by default.

Before you change the setting, the Konjac Translate keyboard won't read your clipboard as soon as you switch to it. It first asks, “New text on your clipboard. Translate it?” Only when you agree does it read the text and trigger the iPhone prompt. That way, you won't get a system prompt just because you wanted to type.

To have a translation ready as soon as you copy text and switch keyboards, go to “Settings” > “Apps” > “Konjac Translate” and set “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow.” Once the keyboard finds that it can read the clipboard without asking, it will translate automatically the next time.

6. Privacy: what the keyboard reads

  • It reads text only when you switch to it.It reads selected text, the text before your cursor, or text you copied if Full Access is on. It uses that text only for translation.
  • It doesn't keep what you type.The keyboard doesn't save, log, or upload your typing.
  • Translation happens on your iPhone.It uses Apple's on-device translation. Once you've downloaded the languages, you don't need an internet connection.
  • AI wording uses Apple Intelligence first, and is generated on your iPhone. If your iPhone doesn't have Apple Intelligence, you can enter your own AI key under “Settings” > “AI” in the app. Only in that case is the text to be rewritten sent directly from your iPhone to the AI service you choose. It doesn't pass through me.
  • Password fields always use the system keyboard.That's an iPhone rule. Custom keyboards don't appear in password fields.

For details, see the “Translation Keyboard” section of the Privacy Policy.

7. Frequently asked questions

Does the translation keyboard cost anything?

No. The translation keyboard and AI wording options are available in the free version. AI wording requires an iPhone with Apple Intelligence or your own AI key entered in the app. If you use your own key, the AI service charges you for its use.

Can I use it without internet?

You can translate offline once you've downloaded the language pair in the app. Apple Intelligence also generates AI wording on your iPhone. You need internet only if you use your own AI key.

Why does it ask for “Allow Full Access”?

It needs Full Access to read the clipboard and connect to your own AI service. You can leave it off if you're translating only selected text or text before the cursor and using Apple Intelligence.

Can I type in another language with the translation keyboard?

No. When there's nothing to translate, it only provides English letters. Use the globe key to return to your usual keyboard, type your message, then switch back to translate it.

What if I choose the wrong translation or wording?

Tap “Undo” at the lower left of the keyboard to bring back your original text.
