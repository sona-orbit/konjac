How to Use an iPhone Translation Keyboard in Any App

Replying to foreign friends no longer requires copying text back and forth between messaging apps and a translation app. With the Konjac Translate keyboard added, simply select the text you just wrote, switch to the keyboard, and the translation is already waiting. Tap “Replace” to swap in their language. When an incoming message is confusing, copy it and switch over. The keyboard displays the meaning along with ready-to-send replies in their language. This guide shows how to set it up, how to use both scenarios, and how to stop the paste prompt from constantly appearing.

1. What the translation keyboard does

The translation keyboard works inside any app. Rather than typing foreign words letter by letter, it translates words already on your screen. It checks three places in order:

  • Text you selected. This is the most accurate method. It translates exactly what you highlight.
  • Text you just copied. Useful for an incoming foreign message.
  • Text before the cursor. Even if you forget to highlight your sentence, the keyboard grabs the words right before your cursor.

It detects language direction automatically. If you write in your language, it translates to their language so you can replace the text. If it spots a foreign language, it translates it into your language for reading without inserting anything into the input field. When all three spots are empty, it acts as a standard English keyboard. To type your native language, tap the globe key to switch back to your usual keyboard.

2. Initial setup: three toggles

Open 翻譯蒟蒻 and go to the “Environment” tab under “Translation Keyboard.” That screen shows a checklist that marks items off as you finish them. Tapping “Open Settings” opens the app settings directly, where you can find these three options:

  1. Add the keyboard: Go to Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard, and select “翻譯蒟蒻.” (the keyboard is listed under its Chinese name)
  2. Turn on “Allow Full Access”: Tap “翻譯蒟蒻” and enable “Allow Full Access.” Reading the clipboard and connecting your own AI keys require this permission. If you only translate highlighted text, you can leave it off.
  3. Set “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow”: Go to Settings > Apps > 翻譯蒟蒻 > Paste from Other Apps. Leaving this on default means your iPhone will ask every time you copy fresh text and switch over (see Section 5).

Once set up, use the practice area at the bottom of that same screen. Tap “Copy sample sentence,” tap the input field, hold the globe key, and pick “Konjac Translate.” You will see the result right away, and the checklist will confirm your setup.

Make sure to download language packs in the app first. The keyboard only translates downloaded language pairs and follows the two languages chosen in the app. To switch targets, tap the language label in the top-left corner of the keyboard.

3. Writing to others: select, switch, and replace

  1. Type what you want to say in your chat box using your regular keyboard.
  2. Select the text (or leave the cursor right at the end of the sentence).
  3. Hold the globe key and choose “Konjac Translate.” The translation appears instantly.
  4. Tap “Replace” to swap your words for the translated text. Check it, then send.

Below the translation, three AI phrasing options appear: Casual (friendly chat tone), Polite (for business contacts or new acquaintances), and Short (concise while keeping the full meaning). Tapping one replaces the text immediately. Tapping another swaps it out cleanly without stacking text. If you tap the wrong one, hit “Undo” in the bottom-left corner to restore your original draft.

If you prefer to show both versions, tap “Insert Below.” Your original draft stays put, and the translation lands on the next line.

4. Reading foreign messages: copy and switch

  1. Press and hold the incoming message, then tap “Copy.”
  2. Tap the input field and switch to the Konjac Translate keyboard.
  3. The keyboard displays the translated meaning. This is for your eyes only and will not paste into the input field, preventing accidental sends.

The “Suggested Replies” area below offers three prewritten responses in the sender’s language, complete with English explanations like “Sounds good,” “Can we reschedule?”, or “Ask a question.” Tap one to insert it into the chat box, tweak it if needed, and send. The keyboard identifies the language automatically. It handles Japanese or Korean messages seamlessly, provided those language models are downloaded.

5. Stopping the constant paste prompts

To protect your clipboard, iOS asks “Allow Paste?” whenever an app attempts to read content copied elsewhere. Keyboards face this rule too, and iOS defaults to asking every time.

Before you adjust this setting, the Konjac Translate keyboard will not read your clipboard silently on open. Instead, it asks: “New text found on clipboard. Translate it?” It only reads the clipboard after you confirm, triggering the iOS prompt. This prevents unexpected popups when you only wanted to type.

To have it translate immediately upon switching, go to Settings > Apps > Konjac Translate, and change “Paste from Other Apps” to “Allow.” Once the keyboard sees that clipboard access is granted without prompts, it translates automatically on switch.

6. Privacy: what the keyboard reads and ignores

  • It reads only when active. It looks at selected text, text before the cursor, or (if Full Access is enabled) copied text, solely for translation purposes.
  • It never logs keystrokes. The keyboard does not store, record, or upload the content you type.
  • Translations run on your iPhone. The app uses Apple on-device models. Once languages are downloaded, it operates fully offline.
  • AI rephrasings rely on Apple Intelligence first, processed locally on your iPhone. If your iPhone lacks Apple Intelligence, you can enter your own AI API key under Environment > AI in the app. Only in that case is text sent directly from your iPhone to your selected provider, completely bypassing me.
  • Password fields always use the system keyboard. iOS disables third-party keyboards in secure password fields automatically.

Read the full details in the Translation Keyboard section of the Privacy Policy.

7. Frequently asked questions

Is the translation keyboard free?

Yes. The translation keyboard and AI rephrasing are free to use. AI rephrasing requires Apple Intelligence on your iPhone or your own AI API key entered in the app (third-party API fees apply directly from your provider).

Does it work offline?

Translation works completely offline once the language pairs are downloaded in the app. Apple Intelligence rephrasing also runs on-device. An internet connection is only needed if you configure external AI API keys.

Why does it require “Allow Full Access”?

Full Access is required to read your clipboard and reach external AI providers. If you only translate highlighted text or text before the cursor using Apple Intelligence, you can use the keyboard without Full Access.

Can I type normally using the translation keyboard?

No. When there is nothing to translate, it only offers a standard Latin keyboard layout. To type in your primary language, tap the globe key to return to your normal keyboard, type your message, and switch back to translate.

What if a translation or replacement goes wrong?

Tap “Undo” in the bottom-left corner of the keyboard to restore your original text.
