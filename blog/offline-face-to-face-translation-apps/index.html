Offline Face-to-Face Translation Apps for iPhone Travel

The short answer: If you only translate a sentence or two now and then, the iPhone’s built-in “Translate” app has a face-to-face view and works offline after you download languages. If you need more languages or want to translate a menu with your camera, try Google Translate. If you say the same things often and want to organize them into cards, try Konjac Translate, the app I made. Whichever you use, download the languages while you still have internet access. This article compares four apps for face-to-face conversations, offline use, saved phrases, and cost. I’ll also be clear about what Konjac Translate cannot do.

How to choose:

  • For a few phrases now and then, without installing another app: Start with the iPhone’s built-in “Translate” app. It also translates with the camera and can show a translation full-screen.
  • For less common languages, or frequent photos of menus and signs: Google Translate’s App Store listing says it translates between 249 languages, though features vary by language. It also offers camera translation.
  • For trips mainly to Korea or Japan: Papago is made by Korea’s NAVER. Its App Store listing says it supports 14 languages, including Korean, Japanese, Chinese, and English.
  • For phrases you say repeatedly and want to organize into cards: That’s what I made Konjac Translate for.

2. Why turn the screen around for face-to-face translation?

At a counter or restaurant table, the awkward part is often turning your phone back and forth. You speak, turn the screen so the other person can read it, then turn it back when they reply.

Konjac Translate’s “Conversation” view splits the screen in half. The half facing the other person is upside down from your perspective. Lay your phone flat between you, and the person across from you can read the translation without turning it.

  • Tap the microphone to speak, or type instead.
  • Translations can be read aloud automatically. You can replay each one.
  • After changing languages, you can translate the previous sentence again.
  • In the free version, ads appear only on your side, not on the half facing the other person.

Apple Translate’s “Face to Face” view and Google Translate’s “Face to face” mode use the same idea: each person reads from their own side. If you already like one of those apps, you may not need to switch.

3. Save phrases you use often as cards

Some things come up almost every day while traveling: your hotel address, an allergy, a food you do not eat, or the station you need. Repeating them into a microphone at every stop gets old, and a noisy room can make speech recognition less reliable.

Konjac Translate lets you save those phrases as cards:

  • Your own decks: Keep travel and work phrases separate. Name your decks and drag cards to reorder them.
  • Save a translation as a card: Save a sentence you just translated or one from your history with a tap.
  • Translate for the person you’re speaking with: Cards translate into your currently selected language, so you can use the same deck in another country.
  • Show it in large type: Display a translated sentence full-screen for a server to read.
  • Built-in travel cards: Common phrases for transportation, dining, lodging, shopping, and directions are ready to use. Tap one to translate it in the card.

Apple Translate lets you save translations to “Favorites,” and Google Translate lets you star them in your phrasebook. That may be enough if you only need to save a few. Konjac Translate’s cards can also be grouped, reordered, and translated again when you select another language.

4. What to download before your trip

Offline translation depends on languages already downloaded to your phone. If you wait until you arrive and have no connection, you cannot download them. Do these things on Wi‑Fi before you leave:

  1. Download languages: In Konjac Translate, open “Offline Languages” in the “Settings” tab and tap “Download” next to the language pair you need. Both languages must be downloaded for offline translation. For Apple Translate, download languages in iPhone “Settings.” In Google Translate, tap the download icon beside a language in the app. Google recommends downloading over Wi‑Fi.
  2. Download the speech model: The first time you use voice input in Konjac Translate, you need Wi‑Fi to download a speech model of about 150–400 MB. On some devices, certain languages support typing but not speech recognition.
  3. Download a voice for spoken playback: If a translation is not read aloud, go to iPhone “Settings” > “Accessibility” > “Spoken Content” > “Voices” and download a voice for that language.
  4. Test it in Airplane Mode: Turn off your connection before the trip. Speak a sentence using the language pair you need and check that it translates and plays aloud.

6. What Konjac Translate cannot do

Check these limits before downloading:

  1. About 20 languages: Konjac Translate uses Apple’s on-device translation, so its languages are limited to those Apple supports. They include Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Hindi, Turkish, Italian, Portuguese, German, French, Spanish, Russian, Ukrainian, Arabic, Polish, and Dutch. Check the language list in the app for what is currently available. If you need another language, I suggest bringing Google Translate too.
  2. No camera translation: Konjac Translate cannot translate photos of menus or signs. Apple Translate, Google Translate, and Papago have camera features if you need one.
  3. iPhone only: It requires iOS 18 or later. There is no Android version.
  4. Downloads required before going offline: You need an internet connection the first time you download languages and speech models.

7. Frequently asked questions

Does Konjac Translate really work without internet?

After you download both languages in a language pair, translation, speech recognition, and spoken playback do not need internet. You need internet to download languages and speech models. Ads in the free version also need internet to appear.

Does the free version have ads?

Yes. A small banner appears at the top of the screen. In the conversation view, it appears only on your side. Watching a video hides ads for 15 minutes. Any Pro plan removes ads.

Is what I say sent off my phone?

Not by default. Translation, speech recognition, and spoken playback happen on your iPhone, and recordings are not saved. The ad service in the free version may receive data such as your IP address and device information, but not what you say, type, or translate. The privacy policy explains exceptions, such as when you choose to report a poor translation.

If my iPhone already has Translate, do I need Konjac Translate?

If you only translate a sentence or two now and then, the built-in “Translate” app may be enough. It has a face-to-face view and offline translation. Konjac Translate adds phrase cards you can group and reorder, built-in travel cards, and, with Pro, flashcard review and conversation summaries.

Do I have to buy Pro again when I get a new iPhone?

No. Sign in with the same Apple Account, open the Pro screen in the app, and tap “Restore Purchases”.
